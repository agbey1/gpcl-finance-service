1Introduction

GPCL Finance Service is Ghana Publishing Company Limited's finance and accounting system. It keeps the general ledger, issues customer invoices with Ghana's statutory levies, records customer receipts, reconciles bank statements and produces the financial statements and levy returns.

About this manual

This manual explains every screen in the system and how to carry out day-to-day finance work. It is written for people who use the system, not for IT staff, so it does not cover installation, servers or databases.

Screen names, buttons and fields are shown in bold blue, for example Create New Invoice. Keyboard keys look like F5. Throughout the manual you will find these boxes:

TipA shortcut or good practice.
NoteBackground that helps you understand what the system is doing.
ImportantSomething that can affect your figures if done wrongly.

What the system does

Sales & receivables
Create tax invoices with VAT, NHIL and GETFund calculated automatically, record customer payments, issue credit notes and void invoices raised in error.
General ledger
Post balanced manual journals (vouchers), keep the chart of accounts, view and reverse journals, and close accounting months.
Banking
Upload bank statements as CSV files, let the system match them to recorded receipts and clear the remaining lines.
Reporting
Dashboard, Day Book, Trial Balance, Profit & Loss, Balance Sheet, Receivables Aging, budgets versus actuals and GRA levy returns.

Key ideas before you start

Everything ends up in the ledger

Every invoice, payment, credit note, void and voucher creates a journal entry in the general ledger at the moment you save it. You never post the same transaction twice: an invoice automatically debits Trade Receivables and credits Sales Revenue and the levy accounts. All reports and dashboard figures are read from these posted journals.

Debits must equal credits

Every journal must balance to the pesewa. The system will not save a voucher whose debits and credits differ.

Corrections, not deletions

Nothing that has been posted is deleted. Mistakes are corrected with a matching opposite entry: an invoice is voided or credited, and a manual journal is reversed. The original and the correction both stay visible, so the audit trail is complete.

Accounting months can be closed

Each calendar month is an accounting period. Once a month is closed, nothing dated in that month can be posted, so reported figures cannot change after the books are signed off.

Document numbers

The system numbers documents automatically, by year:

2Getting Started

Signing in

  1. Open https://finance.gpcllive.com in Google Chrome, Microsoft Edge or Firefox.
  2. Enter your Email Address and Password. Click the eye icon to show the password while you type.
  3. Click Sign In. You arrive on the Dashboard.

Your account is created by a system administrator, who gives you your email address and first password.

If sign-in fails
  • "Invalid email address or password. Please try again." Check the email address and password. Passwords are case-sensitive.
  • "Too many login attempts. Please try again later." After repeated failed attempts sign-in is paused briefly. Wait about a minute and try again.
  • If your account has been deactivated you cannot sign in; contact your administrator.

Your session and signing out

You stay signed in for 8 hours. After that the system returns you to the sign-in page, and any unsaved form is lost, so save your work as you go.

To sign out, click the sign-out icon at the bottom of the left-hand menu, next to your name. Always sign out on shared computers.

Passwords

Passwords must be at least 10 characters long and contain at least one letter and one number. There is no self-service password change: if you forget your password or want to change it, ask a system administrator to reset it (see User Management).

4Chart of Accounts

The chart of accounts is the list of general ledger accounts that every transaction posts to. This screen shows each account with its posted debits, credits and balance.

Viewing accounts

  • Use the type buttons (ALL, ASSET, LIABILITY, EQUITY, REVENUE, EXPENSE) to show one class of account.
  • Type in Search code or name… to find an account.
  • The columns show the code, name, type, category, whether it is a control account, total debits, total credits, balance and status.
  • The bottom row shows the ledger totals and says (balanced) when total debits equal total credits. If it ever says (OUT OF BALANCE), report it to your finance manager.

Control accounts

A control account summarises a detailed sub-ledger, for example Trade Receivables (the total of all customer invoices) or Accounts Payable. Control accounts can only be changed through their own module. Trade Receivables, for instance, moves only through invoices, payments, credit notes and voids. They are therefore not offered in Voucher Entry, which keeps the receivables total equal to the sum of open invoices.

Adding an account

  1. Click New Account.
  2. Enter the Account code (2–20 letters, digits or hyphens, for example 6200) and the Account name.
  3. Choose the account type.
  4. Optionally enter a Category. Categories group accounts in reports. Use exactly these names where they apply:
    • Cash or Bank for cash and bank accounts (Dashboard Bank & Cash tile and financial ratios),
    • Receivables for customer receivables, COGS for cost of goods sold,
    • Inventory, Prepayments, Payables, Tax, Accruals for current assets and current liabilities.
  5. Tick Control account only if the account must never receive manual journals.
  6. Click Create account.
NoteAccount codes and names cannot be edited or deleted from this screen once created, because posted transactions refer to them. If an account is no longer needed, deactivate it.

Deactivating and reactivating an account

Click the Active / Inactive button in the Status column to switch an account. An inactive account no longer accepts postings and is hidden from the account lists in Vouchers and Budgets. If the account still has a balance, the system asks you to confirm first.

ImportantDo not deactivate an account that the system posts to automatically (cash, bank, receivables, revenue or the levy accounts). Invoices and payments would then be refused. These accounts are listed under System Settings → GL Account Mapping.

Opening balances

Opening balances are entered as a Journal Voucher dated on your opening date (see Chapter 5): debit the asset and expense accounts, credit the liability, equity and revenue accounts, so that the voucher balances.

NoteControl accounts, such as Trade Receivables, cannot be posted from Voucher Entry. Ask your system administrator for help loading opening customer balances.

5Voucher Entry

Voucher Entry is where you post manual journals: expenses paid, non-customer receipts, transfers between cash and bank, accruals and adjustments.

Use the right screenCustomer sales, customer receipts and credit notes must be entered on the Invoices & AR and Customer Payments screens, not as vouchers. Those screens keep each customer's balance and the receivables account in step.

Voucher types and shortcuts

Choose the voucher type with the buttons at the top of the screen, or press its function key while on the page:

Posting a voucher

  1. Choose the voucher type.
  2. Set the Voucher date. It defaults to today; the date's month must be open.
  3. Enter a Reference (optional, up to 50 characters, for example a cheque or memo number).
  4. Enter the Narration: what the entry is for (at least 3 characters).
  5. On each line, select the Account, optionally a line description, and an amount in either Debit or Credit. Typing in one column clears the other.
  6. Use Add line for more lines, or the bin icon to remove one. A voucher needs at least two lines.
  7. Check the balance indicator. It reads Balanced when debits equal credits, otherwise Out of balance by GHS ….
  8. Click Post voucher. The button only works when the voucher is complete and balanced.

When the voucher is posted, the screen shows its journal number (for example Posted JNL-2026-000315) with a link to the Journal Entries Register, and clears the form for the next voucher.

Rules the system enforces

  • Only active, non-control accounts can be selected.
  • Each line has exactly one of debit or credit, and amounts cannot be negative.
  • Total debits must equal total credits exactly.
  • The voucher date cannot fall in a closed month. The message is "Financial period M/YYYY is closed. Cannot post journal entries to a closed period."
Made a mistake?A posted voucher cannot be edited. Reverse it from the Journal Entries Register (Chapter 6) and post a corrected voucher.

6Journal Entries Register

The register lists journal entries from every source (invoices, payments, credit notes, voids and vouchers) with their totals and status.

Finding a journal

  • Search by entry number, reference or description.
  • Use the source drop-down to show only one source, for example INVOICE, PAYMENT, CREDIT_NOTE, INVOICE_VOID, MANUAL_PAYMENT, MANUAL_REVERSAL or PAYMENT_REVERSAL.
  • The register shows the most recent 500 entries. For a complete listing over a date range use the Day Book report (Chapter 13).

Status column

StatusMeaning
POSTEDA normal posted entry.
REVERSEDThis entry has been cancelled by a reversal. Hover over it to see which entry reversed it.
REVERSALThis entry is itself a reversal of an earlier entry.

Viewing a journal

Click View to open the entry. You see its date, source, reference, who posted it, the narration and every line with account, description, debit and credit.

Reversing a manual journal

  1. Click Reverse on the entry, in the list or in the opened entry.
  2. Enter the reason when asked (at least 3 characters) and confirm.
  3. The system posts a mirror-image journal dated today and shows its number. The original is marked REVERSED.
Which entries can be reversed?Only manual journals (vouchers) that have not already been reversed and are not themselves reversals. Journals created by invoices, payments, credit notes and voids are corrected through their own screen: void or credit the invoice, or reverse the payment. Because the reversal is dated today, it can be posted even if the original month is closed.

Setting up customers

Every invoice and receipt belongs to a customer. Customers are maintained on the Customers screen, which lists each customer with contact details, GRA TIN, credit limit, the amount currently outstanding and the number of open invoices. Use the ACTIVE / INACTIVE / ALL buttons and the search box to find a customer.

  3. Optionally enter a Credit limit in GHS; leave it blank for no limit.
  4. Click Create customer.

Click Edit to change a customer's details. Click Deactivate for a customer you no longer trade with: their invoices and payments are kept, no new invoices can be raised for them, and their remaining balance can still be paid. Reactivate reverses this. Customers are never deleted.

Creating an invoice

  1. Click Create New Invoice.
  2. Select the Customer / Client.
  3. Check the Invoice date. It defaults to today and can be set to an earlier date in an open month, but not a future date.
  4. Optionally set the Due date. If you leave it blank the invoice is due 30 days after the invoice date. It cannot be earlier than the invoice date.
  5. For each item enter the Item Description, Qty and Unit Price (GHS) before levies. Use + Add Item Row for more items and × to remove one.
  6. Check the totals panel: Net Subtotal, VAT (15%), NHIL (2.5%), GETFund (2.5%) and TOTAL.
  7. Click Post Invoice. The invoice is numbered and posted to the ledger immediately.
CustomersOnly active customers are offered. If a customer is missing, add or reactivate them on the Customers screen first.

Viewing, printing and downloading an invoice

Click View on any invoice to open it as a Tax Invoice showing the company details and TIN, the invoice number and dates, the customer, the line items and the levy breakdown, gross amount and balance due. From here you can:

  • Download PDF: save the invoice as a PDF to email to the customer.
  • Print A4: print the invoice.
  • Void Invoice or Issue Credit Note (below).

Voiding an invoice

Void an invoice that should never have been raised, for example one sent to the wrong customer or duplicated.

  1. Open the invoice and click Void Invoice.
  2. Enter the reason (at least 3 characters) and confirm.

The system posts an entry reversing the receivable, revenue and all three levies, and marks the invoice VOID.

When an invoice cannot be voidedOnly invoices with nothing paid or credited can be voided; the button appears only on UNPAID invoices. Otherwise the message is "Invoice has payments or credit notes applied and cannot be voided. Issue a credit note instead." The void is dated today, so today's month must be open.

Issuing a credit note

Use a credit note to reduce what a customer owes: returned goods, a price adjustment, a discount agreed after invoicing, or a partly wrong invoice.

  1. Open the invoice (it must be Unpaid or Partial with a balance) and click Issue Credit Note.
  2. Enter the amount to credit including levies. It cannot exceed the balance due.
  3. Enter the reason (at least 3 characters).

The system issues a numbered credit note (CN-…), reduces the invoice balance and reverses revenue and each levy in proportion. For example, crediting GHS 120.00 on a 1,200.00 invoice reverses 100.00 of revenue, 15.00 VAT, 2.50 NHIL and 2.50 GETFund. If the credit clears the balance, the invoice becomes PAID.

8Customer Payments & Receipts

Record money received from customers, apply it to their invoices, and print official receipts.

The payments screen

  • Total Received: all receipts listed.
  • Allocated to Invoices: receipts applied to a specific invoice.
  • Unallocated: receipts held on the customer's account (prepayments).

Filter by method (Bank Transfer, Cheque, Mobile Money, Cash) or search by receipt number, customer, reference or invoice. Export the list with Excel or PDF.

Recording a payment

  1. Click Record Payment.
  2. Select the Customer / Client. Inactive customers are listed with "(inactive)" so their remaining balances can still be paid.
  3. In Apply to invoice, choose the invoice being paid. Only that customer's open invoices are listed, with their balances. Choose Unallocated (payment on account) if the payment is not for a specific invoice.
  4. Enter the Amount (GHS) and the Payment date. The date cannot be in the future, and its month must be open.
  5. Choose the Payment method and enter the Reference / Cheque #, such as the transfer reference, cheque or Mobile Money transaction number.
  6. Click Record Payment & Post GL.

The invoice balance reduces immediately and its status becomes PARTIAL or PAID.

Where the money is postedBank Transfer receipts are debited to the bank account. Cash, Cheque and Mobile Money receipts are debited to the cash account, as the form indicates. When cheques or Mobile Money receipts are later banked, record the transfer with a Contra Voucher (Chapter 5). In every case Trade Receivables is credited.

Overpayments and payments on account

  • Overpayment. If the amount is more than the invoice balance, the form warns you. On saving, the invoice is marked Paid and the excess is recorded as an open credit note (CN-OVERPAY-…) for the customer.
  • On account. An unallocated payment reduces the customer's overall receivable without settling a particular invoice.
ImportantA recorded payment cannot be edited. Check the customer, invoice and amount before saving. A payment recorded wrongly is corrected by reversing it (below) and recording it again.

Printing an official receipt

Click Receipt on any payment to display an Official Receipt showing the receipt number and date, who paid, the method, the reference, what it was applied to and the amount. Click Print Receipt to print it.

Reversing a payment

Reverse a payment that was recorded in error (wrong customer, invoice or amount) or a cheque that has bounced.

  1. Click Receipt on the payment, then Reverse Payment.
  2. Enter the reason (at least 5 characters, for example "Cheque 000123 returned unpaid") and confirm.

The system then:

  • posts a reversing ledger entry dated today that exactly cancels the payment's original entry;
  • puts the amount back on the invoice, which returns to UNPAID or PARTIAL;
  • cancels any overpayment credit (CN-OVERPAY) the payment created;
  • marks the payment REVERSED. It stays in the list, struck through, and is left out of the totals and of bank statement matching.

Reversing requires the Reverse Payments permission (Finance Administrators and Senior Accountants by default). A payment can be reversed only once, and not if its overpayment credit has already been used.

Payments that cannot be applied are refused with clear messages, for example "Invoice is already fully paid. Cannot apply additional payments." or "Invoice is voided. Cannot process payment on voided invoices."

9Bank Reconciliation

Bank reconciliation confirms that the money on your bank statement agrees with what was recorded in the system. Upload the statement and the system matches what it can automatically; you then clear the rest.

Choosing the bank account

The cards at the top list the company's active bank accounts, each with its general ledger account, bank name, account number and current GL Balance. Click a card to work on that account.

Preparing the statement file

Download the statement from internet banking as a CSV file, or save it from Excel as "CSV (Comma delimited)". The file must:

  • have a header row and at least one transaction row, and be no larger than 10 MB;
  • contain a date column, a description column, and either separate debit/credit columns or a single signed amount column (negative = money out).

Column headings are recognised flexibly, so most bank downloads work without editing:

Dates may be written 2026-09-30 or day-first, such as 30/09/2026 or 30-09-26.

Day-first datesDates like 03/09/2026 are read as 3 September. If your file uses month-first dates, change the date format in Excel to dd/mm/yyyy before saving.

Uploading and automatic matching

  1. Click Upload CSV Statement.
  2. Check the Target Bank Account and choose the file.
  3. Click Ingest & Auto-Match Statement. A message reports how many lines were matched and how many remain unmatched.

For each statement line the system looks for a recorded customer payment of the same amount (within GHS 1.00) dated between six months before and 30 days after the statement date. If none is found, it looks for a posted journal line of the same amount in the same window. Each payment can only be matched once, and reversed payments are never matched. Matched lines are marked CLEARED and show the matched reference.

Clearing unmatched lines

Lines marked UNMATCHED need your attention. Common causes and actions:

CauseWhat to do
Bank charge, interest or levy not yet recordedPost a Payment or Receipt Voucher for it, then clear the line with Resolve Variance.
Customer receipt not yet recordedRecord it on Customer Payments; the next statement upload will match it.
Timing difference (recorded in a different period)Clear with Resolve Variance, stating "timing difference".

To clear a line, click Resolve Variance, enter the reason (at least 3 characters, for example "bank charge September") and confirm.

NoteResolving a variance records your explanation; it does not post anything to the ledger. If money actually left or entered the bank, post the matching voucher as well.

10Budgets vs. Actuals

Set annual budgets for income and expense accounts and track actual performance against them.

Creating a budget

  1. Select the fiscal year (FY) at the top right: next year, this year or one of the two previous years.
  2. Click New Budget and enter a Budget name and, optionally, a description.
  3. For each account to budget, choose the account (active revenue and expense accounts are listed) and enter the annual amount. Use Add account for more lines.
  4. Click Create budget.
NoteBudgets are annual and cannot be changed after they are created. To revise a budget, create a new one with a clear name, for example "2027 Operating Budget – Revised June".

Reviewing performance

Select a budget from the list on the left. For each account the table shows the budget, the actual posted in that fiscal year, the variance and a consumption bar with the percentage used. Overspent expenses and revenue below target are highlighted as unfavourable. The bottom row totals the budget.

Actual figures include only posted ledger entries dated within the selected year.

11Fiscal Period Close

Closing a month locks it: no invoice, payment, credit note, void or voucher dated in that month can be posted afterwards.

The period close screen

Choose the year at the top right. For each month the table shows its date range, the number of journals, total debits and credits, its status (OPEN or CLOSED) and who closed it and when.

Closing a month

  1. Complete the month-end checklist (Chapter 15).
  2. Click Close on the month. The button is available only after the month has ended.
  3. Confirm. The system checks that the month's ledger balances; if it does not, it refuses and shows the debit and credit totals.

Every close is recorded in the System Audit Logs.

Before you closeMake sure all invoices, receipts and vouchers for the month are in, the bank is reconciled and the levy return has been checked. After closing, corrections can only be posted in an open month.

Re-opening a closed month

A closed month can be re-opened only by a Super Administrator. A Re-open button appears on closed months for them.

  1. Click Re-open on the month.
  2. Enter the reason (at least 10 characters). It is recorded in the audit log.
  3. Make the correction, then close the month again.

12Ghana GRA Levy Returns

This screen gives the output VAT, NHIL and GETFund figures for each month, ready for your GRA filing.

Reading the screen

  • The three tiles show the VAT, NHIL and GETFund payable for the last complete month.
  • The monthly levy register covers the last six complete months: taxable sales, each levy, total levies, and the number of invoices and credit notes.
  • Figures come from the ledger, so voided invoices and credit notes are already deducted.
  • The Check column shows OK when each levy equals its rate × taxable sales, or REVIEW when they differ, for instance if a manual voucher was posted to a levy account. Investigate any REVIEW month before filing.

Use Excel or PDF to export the register. For any other date range, run the Levy Return report in Financial Reports.

ScopeThese are output levies on sales only. Input VAT on purchases and withholding tax are not tracked in the system and must be worked out separately.

14Administration

These screens are for system administrators: managing users, roles and permissions, reviewing the audit trail and maintaining settings.

User Management

The list shows every user with their name, email, role, status and when they last signed in (GMT). "Never" means the user has not signed in yet.

Adding a user

  1. Click Add New User and enter the Full Name and Email Address.
  2. Choose the Assigned System Role (see Roles & Permissions).
  3. Enter an Initial Account Password and confirm it. It must be at least 10 characters with a letter and a number.
  4. Save, and give the user their email address and password securely.

Changing a user

  • Change role: pick a new role from the drop-down in the user's row.
  • Activate / deactivate: use the status button. A deactivated user cannot sign in, but their history is kept.
  • Edit or reset password: click the edit icon to change the name, email or status, or type a new password to reset it.
Super Administrator accountsOnly a Super Administrator can make someone a Super Administrator, or edit, reset or deactivate a Super Administrator's account. The last active Super Administrator cannot be demoted or deactivated, so the system always has one.

Role and permission changes take effect the next time the user signs in.

Roles & Permissions

A role is a named set of permissions. Every user has exactly one role.

Built-in roles

RoleIntended for
Super AdministratorFull access, including re-opening closed periods and managing other Super Administrators. Not shown in the roles list; assigned from User Management.
Finance AdministratorFull access to all finance work and administration. Its permissions cannot be edited.
Senior AccountantJournals, invoices, voids, payments, period close and bank reconciliation.
AR ClerkCreating invoices and recording customer payments.
Financial AuditorRead-only access to the ledger, journals, invoices, payments and audit logs.

Built-in roles cannot be renamed or deleted. Appendix A lists exactly what each one can do.

Creating a custom role

  1. Click Create New Role and enter a name (letters, numbers, spaces and - _ & . ( ) only) and a description. Names of built-in roles cannot be reused.
  2. The new role is created with no permissions and selected for you.
  3. Tick the permissions it needs in the panel on the right, grouped by Accounting, Invoicing & AR, Banking and Administration.
  4. Click Save Permissions.

To change an existing role's permissions, select it, tick or untick permissions and click Save Permissions. Custom roles can be renamed (users keep the role) or deleted once no users are assigned to them. All role changes are recorded in the audit log.

System Audit Logs

The audit trail records who posted, voided, reversed or changed what, and when, including invoices, payments, credit notes, journals, period closes and re-opens, users, roles and settings.

  • Set the From and To dates and, optionally, an entity type, then refresh.
  • Filter further by user, action or details using the search box.
  • Click a row to see the Before and After values of the change.

Audit entries cannot be edited or deleted by anyone.

Appendix BGlossary

TermMeaning
Accounts receivable (AR)Money owed to the company by customers for invoices not yet paid.
AgingGrouping of unpaid invoices by how many days past their due date they are.
Chart of accountsThe list of all general ledger accounts.
Contra entryA transfer between two of the company's own cash or bank accounts.
Control accountA ledger account that summarises a sub-ledger (such as all customer balances) and can only be posted through that sub-ledger.
Credit noteA document that reduces the amount a customer owes on an invoice.
Debit / CreditThe two sides of every entry. Assets and expenses increase with debits; liabilities, equity and revenue increase with credits.
Fiscal periodA calendar month in which transactions are recorded; it can be closed.
GETFund levyGhana Education Trust Fund levy, 2.5% of the net sale value.
General ledger (GL)The complete record of all the company's accounts and postings.
Journal entryA balanced set of debit and credit lines recorded in the general ledger.
NarrationThe description explaining what a journal or voucher is for.
NHILNational Health Insurance Levy, 2.5% of the net sale value.
Output leviesVAT, NHIL and GETFund charged to customers on sales, payable to GRA.
PostingRecording a transaction in the general ledger.
ReconciliationAgreeing the bank statement with the company's own records.
ReversalAn entry that exactly cancels an earlier manual journal.
Trial balanceList of all account balances, used to confirm total debits equal total credits.
VATValue Added Tax, 15% of the net sale value.
VoidCancellation of an unpaid invoice, reversing all its ledger entries.
VoucherA manual journal entry (payment, receipt, contra or journal voucher).
